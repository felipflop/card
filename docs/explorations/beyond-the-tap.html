Beyond the tap

Every idea here keeps the card passive: no battery, nothing to charge. Each one was checked against the card's real limits and, where possible, against published research or a shipping product. The readiness tag says how much of it has been proven by someone else and how much would be a first.

All ideas

Solar power

NFC gives up to 10 mW, but only for the second a phone is held there. A thin solar cell gives a few hundred µW all day. Store that, and the card stops depending on the phone for power.

The biggest win isn't the solar cell itself. With stored energy, the tag can run from VCC and flag an arriving phone on GPIO2 (GPIO2Notif = NFC field notification, datasheet Table 24). The MCU wakes on that pin and lights the LEDs on every tap, including a plain iPhone background read. That removes the ManageGPIO limitation from the current design.

Storage, still battery-free

MLCC bank (in the schematic): about 100 µF holds only ~30 µJ usable between 1.8 V and the MCU's 1.62 V floor. Enough for a fade, not for a show.

CAP-XX thin prismatic supercap: the DMH line is 0.4 mm thick, 40 mF at 4.5 V (two cells). Run from 4.5 V down to 1.8 V through the PMIC, that's about 340 mJ: three minutes of one LED at full brightness, or days of standby. It fits the 0.43 mm stiffener pocket with almost nothing to spare.

Power management

e-peas AEM10330: cold-starts from 275 mV and 3 µW, regulated 1.2–3.3 V output, four external parts. Best cold start of the three.

TI BQ25570: 488 nA quiescent, MPPT; sources disagree on its cold start (330 vs 600 mV), so the TI datasheet decides.

Nexperia NEH2000BY: 35 µW–2 mW input, 2.5–4.5 V storage, aimed at lithium; supercap use needs confirming.

Package heights for all three still need checking against the pocket.

The antenna risk

Every one of these cells has a continuous back electrode, and it sits inside the coil, which is the card's whole flux window. A conductive sheet there acts like a shorted turn: it steals field and detunes the coil, the same reason the plan bans metallized films over the coil.

Ways around it: keep the cell to a few cm², use a module made of series strips so the back metal is slit, and measure a coupon on the VNA before committing. I'd treat this as the gate for the whole solar idea.

What the extra power buys

  • P1Electrochromic tap counter: ~1 mJ per cm² per update. A 10 cm² a-Si or OPV cell at 500 lx makes about 200 µW, so it can refresh a 2 cm² display every 10 s from light alone.
  • P2Daylight twinkle: a 1 ms, 1 mA blink is about 2 µJ. The same cell could blink every 10 ms; a gentle pulse every few seconds costs almost nothing.
  • S1Always-listening LEDs: the card can sample ambient light between taps and greet a phone screen without waiting for NFC power.
  • newLEDs on every tap, no app: stored energy plus GPIO2 field notification. Costs one MCU pin, which arrives anyway with the 20-pin package P1 needs.

Looked at, ruled out

Thermochromic ink driven by a heater

A paper-based leuco display needed about 50 mW per cm² to switch. The whole card harvests at most 10 mW, so a heated patch would be tiny and slow. Body-heat thermochromic ink in the skin is still fine, since it needs no power. source

Invisible infrared tags

InfraredTags (CHI '22) hides codes that only an IR camera sees. Phones have IR cameras for face unlock, but apps and browsers can't use them, so nobody tapping the card could read one. source

FIDO / passkey login

The NTAG X DNA datasheet lists AES and ECC authentication and SIGMA-I, but no FIDO/CTAP applet. A custom challenge-response login (C2 below) works; a browser passkey prompt won't.

E-ink and supercaps

Both are too thick for a 0.76 mm stack, and e-ink needs far more than 1.8 V to switch. The electrochromic display (P1) covers the same need at 0.2–0.35 mm and 1.5 V.
